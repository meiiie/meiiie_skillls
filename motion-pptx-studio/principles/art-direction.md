# art-direction
Art direction is decided before slide 1, on a board, and every rule has a reason in the concept.
- **A palette per chapter.** Give each chapter its own ground and accent (night navy, cream
  paper, lava, ocean...), ordered so the sequence itself says something ("the deck redshifts as
  the letters get older"). Role names stay fixed in ds.py and a theme fills them, so films use it too.
- **Every slide has a background.** That means a full-bleed real photo, or a flat editorial
  illustration that fills the frame (cross-section, cutaway, dial, ruler, map, board). A flat
  ground with only text is allowed for the sources slide alone. Films sit on their chapter's ground.
  Even then the ground is a material, not a fill (surface-and-depth: scripts/surface.py).
- **One picture per hero number.** Each big number gets its own visual metaphor (7.5 loops round
  the Earth, an envelope stretched 15.44x, a clock dial, a pie wedge, shells), never a stat card.
- **A deliberate type pairing, chosen for the concept.** A display serif for the voice, a heavy
  condensed for huge numerals (150-230 pt), a mono for metadata, a plain sans for body.
  Auto-fit display type to its box: measure the text width with the real font file.
- **Illustrations are editorial, not clip-art.** A native or drawn illustration fills the frame: it bleeds,
  has 3+ value planes (foreground, subject, ground) and a light direction. Rows of identical small icons
  (lollipop trees, blocks, dots) are diagrams, not art.
- **One recurring motif with meaning**, used quietly (a rail, a mark).
- **Vary composition** per composition-range.
- **Board, then critique.** Render palettes, type, motif and 30 wireframe tiles into one image.
  Compare it with the best reference you have and fix the plan before building.
