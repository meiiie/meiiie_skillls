# art-analysis
Before designing, study 2-4 references that are genuinely good (the user's, examples/, strong
published decks or posters). Study them to learn WHY they work and HOW they were made. Never
copy their palettes, layouts or motifs (examples-are-a-yardstick-not-a-mold).
- **Make an annotated board.** Put crops of good references beside weak ones (your own earlier
  drafts count). Tag each crop GOOD, WEAK or DECOR, and caption it with one cause.
  Save the board in design/ and look at it.
- **Ask the same questions every time:**
  - temperature: does it shift between chapters?
  - value: does the hero still win in greyscale?
  - accent: is there one accent colour with one job?
  - type scale: how big is the ratio from the biggest to the smallest type (great decks reach
    15-25x)? How many voices are there?
  - crop and negative space: is there a bold crop? Does the empty space point somewhere?
  - surface: what material is it, and where does the light come from?
  - images: are they graded, cut out and grounded, or just put in boxes?
  - rhythm: does the composition change from slide to slide in the contact-sheet row?
- **Separate meaningful from decorative.** A trait is meaningful when it comes from the subject:
  kraft paper and polaroids for a case file, a scroll and ink wash for classical thought. It is a
  cliché when it is applied to anything: gingham, stickers, glow frames, diamond masks, photo-filled
  letters, grunge.
- **Turn findings into decisions.** Write each one as "cause -> what I will do -> which slides".
  Put the list in design/directions.md, before three-directions.
- **Re-run the questions on your contact sheet** in look-then-fix. Any slide that fails two or more
  questions gets redone.
