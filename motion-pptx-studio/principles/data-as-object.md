# data-as-object
A default chart is evidence. Data turned into an object is a picture people remember.
- **Find the number's body.** Ask what the quantity physically is: a share of the sky, rice stacked in
  bowls, a road you could drive, a queue of people, a wall that rises. Draw the number AS that thing,
  at hero scale, and let it bleed when it is large.
- **One insight per data slide.** Write the finding as the title. Annotate the ONE point that proves it
  (a label on the bar, a ring on the year). Everything else gets quieter: no gridlines, no legend,
  thin axis or none.
- **Default bars and lines are allowed at most once per chapter**, and even then they are designed:
  the chapter palette, one accent bar, values on the marks, the axis dropped, the chart filling the frame.
- **Compare with scale, not with columns.** Two quantities can be two objects whose real size ratio is
  visible (a 3.19 m car beside a 4.5 m turning circle), or one number at 260 pt beside its unit.
- **Lists are not slides.** Use three-column or numbered text lists at most once per chapter. Otherwise
  the idea becomes an image, a sequence or a film beat.
- **Never reuse a picture.** A film's end state, an isometric scene or a photo appears on one slide only.
  The next slide moves the camera or changes the idea. art_check FAILS near-duplicate slides.
- **No number on an empty card.** A big numeral alone on a plain field is a card, not an object: put it on
  its photo, make it the thing's size, or give it a scale partner. The critic lists card slides; redo them.
