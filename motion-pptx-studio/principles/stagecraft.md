# stagecraft
Distilled from Nancy Duarte (slide:ology, Resonate, the Glance Test), Garr Reynolds (Presentation Zen),
Apple keynote practice as reported by Carmine Gallo, and OpenPresentation's slide criteria. Only what the
other principles do not already say. WHY, not templates.
- **Glance test (Duarte): 3 seconds.** Look at a tile, look away, say its point. If you cannot, the slide holds
  two ideas or no hierarchy: split it or cut it. The critic runs this on every tile.
- **One number, one slide (Apple).** Two statistics worth remembering get two slides ("6 million", then
  "71 seconds · sold out"). A slide with three hero numbers has none.
- **Picture and words say the same thing (picture superiority).** The photo must SHOW the title's claim,
  not decorate the topic. A server hall under "who owns your data" is decoration; a hand holding a phone
  that shows a location history is the claim.
- **Sparkline structure (Duarte).** The talk alternates "what is" and "what could be"; each swing is a
  visual swing too (dark/light, wide/close, crowded/empty), ending on a call to action, not a summary.
- **Area is priority.** The most important thing is the largest thing; size, not colour, carries hierarchy.
  It must read at thumbnail size, because decks are shared as screenshots.
- **Signal over noise (Reynolds).** No logo on every slide, no footer bars, no decorative rules or labels
  that repeat on every page; empty space is active, not unused.
- **Optical craft (OpenPresentation).** One spacing unit and its multiples; shared edges and baselines;
  adjust what looks off even when the numbers say it is aligned; no orphaned single word on a line.
