# Academic research delivery

## Define the scholarly outcome

Separate literature review, question formation, research design, analysis, replication, and manuscript writing. Capture discipline, research question, intended audience or venue, time window, methods constraints, data access, and the output requested. Do not promise novelty, acceptance, publication tier, or an “A*” result before evidence and review.

Use useful roles selectively: subject researcher, methods/statistics specialist, literature verifier, replication engineer, or critical reviewer. A critical reviewer should challenge methods and claims using the evidence, not merely polish prose.

## Build an auditable evidence base

Search current primary literature and authoritative sources for claims that depend on them. Record search scope and dates, inclusion/exclusion criteria when systematic coverage is requested, and relevant competing results. Open and verify cited sources; distinguish title/abstract-only access from reading the full text.

For material claims record: claim, exact source, identifier or stable link, passage or result supporting it, study context, and limitations. Prefer original studies over secondary summaries when available. Verify author, year, title, venue, and DOI or other identifier before citation. If a source is inaccessible, state what was and was not verified rather than inventing details.

Perform a novelty check against the closest prior work: overlap, difference, why it could matter, contrary evidence, and remaining search uncertainty. Describe an apparent gap as provisional, with search date and scope. Publication decisions belong to the venue; a workflow cannot guarantee them.

## Design and analyze

Separate established findings, interpretations, hypotheses, proposals, and simulations. Label synthetic data and simulated results prominently; never present them as observed evidence. Identify dataset provenance, permission, sample construction, measurement definitions, leakage risks, and ethical or human-subject review needs.

For computational or empirical results, preserve reproducible methods, code/version information, parameters, randomness controls where applicable, and checks supporting the analysis. Assess uncertainty, assumptions, robustness, appropriate baselines, and alternative explanations. Avoid causal language unsupported by the design.

Do not fabricate data, experiments, sources, reviewer endorsements, or literature coverage. Lack of access or a null result is a valid finding.

## Review and deliver

Audit claim-to-citation support and the main inference independently where feasible. Review whether conclusions follow from methods and evidence and whether limitations are prominent enough. Deliver the requested artifact with verified references, methods, evidence gaps, and concrete next experiments or searches. Keep editorial readiness separate from scientific validity and submission readiness.
